Candidate custom shot flow and integration mapping
- NativeCareerLayout.h has a separate Power Shots rank screen and starts the first situation at the chosen rank; the native layout is source-tested but this pass does not claim loaded-game acceptance.
- plugin/include/TrainingLessonInput.h defines TrainingHudModel from bridge coaching fields including scenario index/title/rank/time/countdown. plugin/src/CareerUI.cpp::renderTrainingHud (around 700–745) draws a custom top-left situation/title/rank panel, timer branch, and D-pad hints for Power Shots. When countdown is GO, that branch displays GO instead of the numeric time branch. src/my_career_training/game_worker.py::Session.render separately draws instruction/timing/target overlays; these are app-owned renderers, not proof of stock top-left treatment.
- The custom engine uses rank-specific 9–15 second timeout settings in training/scenarios.py and ends an attempt on timeout in training/engine.py. Timeout publishes the attempt/result; it does not request a ball hide/fade/destroy. With auto-reset enabled, game_worker.py waits for the attempt acknowledgement plus one second and calls its ordinary full-state reset; retry uses the same reset path. This resets the configured car/ball/bot state rather than demonstrating stock timeout disappearance.
- In-game custom “next situation” sends training_scenario_step; game_worker.py replaces the scenario and controller state, writes desired_state and resets actors. It is not a traced call to native HandleNextGame or IncrementRound. Initial custom placement/launch comes from the RLBot match/desired-state path, separate from PlayTraining/TrainingEditor.
- No product source calls BallWrapper FadeOutBall/FadeInBall/DestroyBall or TrainingEditorWrapper HandleNextGame/StartPlayTest. Exact stock top-left HUD class, controller hint source, timer start/pause/expiry rules, timeout ball visibility behavior and stock setup/launch/retry/next event chain remain unknown from the available evidence. These need a focused package/source extraction or an explicitly owned runtime capture before being called verified.
