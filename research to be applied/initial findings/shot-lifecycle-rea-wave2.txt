### What these artifacts establish

- The older `TrainingPlugin.dll` imports `bakkesmod.dll` wrappers including `ServerWrapper::GetBall`, `ServerWrapper::GenerateShot`, actor location/velocity/rotation/stop setters/getters, `GameWrapper::IsInCustomTraining`, and `GameWrapper::SetTimeout`. Its strings include `shot_initial_ball_*`, `shot_waitbeforeshot`, `shot_countdowntime`, `shot_reset`, `shot_load`, and `shot_generate`; the reset/load/generate command descriptions in this artifact say “deprecated.” This is evidence of a BakkesMod workshop-shot plugin surface, not the stock in-game TrainingEditor timeout path.
- The current `MyCareerTraining.dll` imports `pluginsdk.dll` wrappers for `GetBall`, `GetGameTimeRemaining`, `GetbRoundActive`, `GetGameStateTimeRemaining`, `GetReplicatedGameStateTimeRemaining`, `IsFinished`, `SetGameStateTimeRemaining2`, `GameWrapper::SetTimeout`, and Gfx training methods including `PlayTraining`, `GetTrainingType`, `GetCurrentRound`, `GetTotalRounds`, and `GetDifficulty`. It also imports `XInputGetState` and `USER32!SendInput`. The import table shows names present in the PE; it does not show that any are reached in a particular shot or timeout path. `SetGameStateTimeRemaining2` is a setter and must not be used by an observation probe.
- MyCareerTraining strings include its own `mct_training_next`, `mct_training_retry`, `retry_lesson`, `training_scenario_step`, `timeout_seconds`, `training_progress`, and `pause.training_*` labels. They identify app-owned controls/state labels, not proof of the native TrainingEditor UI or ball-timeout behavior.
- Neither complete import table contains a named `FadeOutBall`, `FadeInBall`, fade/hide getter, `DestroyBall`, `ResetBalls`, `HandleNextGame`, `StartNewRound`, or playtest lifecycle import. A bounded printable ASCII/UTF-16LE scan of those same two DLLs found none of the exact fade/hide/reset identifiers either. That does not exclude inline/reflection paths or prove these APIs are unused elsewhere.
- REA’s Ghidra-defined string inventory for TrainingPlugin matched only `shot_waitbeforeshot`, `shot_countdowntime`, `shot_reset`, `shot_load` and `shot_generate` from the requested pattern; it found no fade/hide/reset identifiers. Its limitation is explicit: only Ghidra-defined string data is observed, so empty search results do not prove the bytes or call paths are absent. Ghidra reported a `TrainingPlugin.pdb` reference but load state unknown; no PDB was supplied or loaded.

## What the SDK names do and do not prove

Pinned declarations are in `<private research>/BakkesModSDK/include/bakkesmod/wrappers`:

- `GameObject/BallWrapper.h:23-24` declares `void FadeOutBall()` and `void FadeInBall()`. These are mutation methods; their declarations show that a caller can request ball fade operations, not that stock timeout calls either one.
- `BallWrapper.h:33-38` declares getters/setters for `bEndOfGameHidden`, `bFadeIn`, and `bFadeOut`. The getters return `unsigned long`. Their names suggest state fields, but the header alone does not establish the meaning, timing, visibility result, replication, or relationship to shot timeout. `BallWrapper.h:92-94,103` separately declares `SetEndOfGameHidden`, `DoDestroy`, and `TurnOff`; these are also not evidence of the stock timeout route.
- A safe observer can check `ActorWrapper::IsNull`/`operator bool`, then read `GetLocation`, `GetVelocity`, `GetRotation`, and `GetbHidden` (`Engine/ActorWrapper.h:14-27,56-58`). `ServerWrapper::GetBall`, `GetGameTimeRemaining`, and `GetbRoundActive` are declared at `GameEvent/ServerWrapper.h:21,61-72`. Game-event clocks and completion are `GetGameStateTimeRemaining`, `GetReplicatedGameStateTimeRemaining`, and `IsFinished` (`GameEvent/GameEventWrapper.h:73-76,125`). The SDK does not say which clock is the stock shot countdown.
- Built-in training context is available through `gfx/GfxDataTrainingWrapper.h:35-42`: training type, score, current/total round, difficulty, tutorial tip, and per-shot progress. These are context getters, not a documented stock shot timer.
- The separate editor lifecycle surface declares `DestroyBall` (`GameEvent/TrainingEditorWrapper.h:52`), `SetRoundTimeLimit`, `HandleNextGame`, and `ResetBalls` (`:62-64`), round increment/spawn/setup/switch (`:76,79-81`), and restart/end/start playtest plus `StartNewRound` (`:83-89`). No artifact evidence here maps a stock UI action to those methods or establishes call order.

## Minimum read-only timeout/reset probe

For one selected built-in training/shot, sample only getters and event metadata at a monotonic timestamp. Record a baseline on entry, ball setup, launch/active play, immediately before and after the countdown reaches zero, any hide/fade transition, retry/reset, next shot, and teardown:

1. Training context: type, difficulty, current/total round, progress-per-shot; capture both available time values (`ServerWrapper::GetGameTimeRemaining` and `GameEventWrapper` game-state/replicated time) plus `GetbRoundActive`/`IsFinished`. This distinguishes clocks without assuming one drives the shot timeout.
2. Ball presence and identity: `GetBall` result and `IsNull`/validity first; if present, preserve a stable object identity if the observer can expose one, then sample `GetbHidden`, `GetbEndOfGameHidden`, `GetbFadeIn`, `GetbFadeOut`, location, velocity, and rotation. Log explicit “ball unavailable” rows rather than skipping those samples.
3. At retry/reset and next-shot boundaries, capture the same fields before and after. This reveals whether the ball remains the same object, is moved/restored, disappears, or is replaced, and whether the relevant timer/round state resets.

Getter polling can prove state changes and ordering relative to observed events; it cannot prove that a particular native function caused them. If call attribution is required, a separate passive function-entry trace for the specific fade/hide/destroy and editor-transition methods must be captured and kept distinct from the getter snapshots. Do not call fade, setters, destroy, reset, playtest, or time-changing APIs during the observer run. No stock TrainingEditor launch/timeout/retry/next runtime trace was captured in this wave.
